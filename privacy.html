Squeeze — Privacy Policy

Effective October 26, 2026

The short version

Squeeze itself collects nothing about you. There are no accounts, no sign-up and no analytics, and the game plays fully offline. From version 2.0, Squeeze shows occasional ads through Google AdMob, which receives limited device data to deliver them; from version 2.2, at the end of every second or third match and when you change the question more than twice in a match. Ads are non-personalized, Squeeze never asks to track you, and nothing is for sale inside the app. From version 2.1, Squeeze also downloads a small settings file from this website when it starts. Version 2.1 alone could also play an ad you chose to watch for a reward and, only if you asked, remind you about the Daily Question with notifications scheduled on your device; version 2.2 removed both. Versions before 2.0 show no ads and contain no ad software.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The Google Mobile Ads SDK runs inside the app. In version 2.1 only, you could also choose to watch a rewarded ad (to repair a streak, for an extra Rain Check, or to change the question again): it played only when you tapped a button marked AD, with the same Google SDK, the same non-personalized settings and the same kinds of data as the other ads. Version 2.2 has no rewarded ads. To show and measure ads and to prevent fraud, Google may receive your IP address (which indicates your approximate location), device identifiers, information about the ads you see and tap, and app diagnostics such as crash and performance data. Squeeze requests non-personalized ads only: ads are not based on your interests or your activity in other apps, Squeeze never asks for permission to track you, and ads are limited to content suitable for all ages. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google’s consent tool asks for your choice before any ad; you can change it at any time with the Privacy choices button on Squeeze’s home screen. Google processes this data under its own policies: Google Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Settings file (version 2.1 and later)

When Squeeze starts, it downloads a small settings file from this website (jjfisherdev.github.io/squeeze-support/config/v1.json) that can switch features on or off and tune the game, such as how often ads appear or whether VS AI is offered. The request contains no identifier, no account and nothing about you or your games. As with any website, the site’s host, GitHub, receives your device’s IP address and standard request information (such as the time of the request) to deliver the file, and may keep them in its logs for security under the GitHub General Privacy Statement. We do not receive those logs. If the download fails, Squeeze simply uses its built-in settings.

Reminders (version 2.1 only)

After your first Daily Question, version 2.1 asked whether you would like a reminder; iOS asked for notification permission only if you said yes. Reminders were local notifications: Squeeze scheduled them on your device, with that day’s question as the text, at most one a day and never between 10 p.m. and 8 a.m. No push service or server was involved and nothing was sent anywhere. Version 2.2 has no reminders and never asks for notification permission; when it starts, it removes any reminder that version 2.1 had scheduled.

Sharing a Daily Question result (version 2.1 only)

In version 2.1, SHARE opened Apple’s share sheet with one line of text, for example “Squeeze Daily #12 · inside ±8% · 🔥 5”: your result and your streak, never the answer. You chose where it went, or cancelled. Squeeze itself sent nothing and received nothing. Version 2.2 has no Daily Question and no sharing.

Age assurance

Some places require apps to respect a player’s age and a parent’s approval (for example Texas, from June 2026). Squeeze asks Apple whether such a law applies to you, and only if it does, iOS asks whether to share your age range (for example 13 to 15) with Squeeze through Apple’s Declared Age Range feature. The age range stays on your device and is never sent to us. Squeeze uses it for two things only: to tell Google to treat ad requests as a child’s or a teen’s (Google receives that instruction, not your age range), and, when a parent must approve app changes, to keep ads off until the parent approves through Apple. Squeeze remembers only whether that approval was given.

Location (“Near You” mode)

If you choose the optional Near You mode, Squeeze asks for while-using location permission so it can pick trivia questions about places around you. Your coordinates are compared against a places database that ships inside the app, on your device. Your location is used only in the app's working memory, and only in that mode: it is never saved to your device, never transmitted, and never shared with anyone, including us and Google. Declining or revoking permission simply switches the app to Random mode.

Data stored on your device

Squeeze keeps a small amount of data locally so the game works: your language, your Random or Near You choice and match length, whether you have watched the demo round (and, from version 2.1, whether the app was newly downloaded or updated from an earlier version, so an update never replays it), the counters that space out ads (matches played, matches since the last ad and how many to wait, the time of the last ad) and, where age assurance applies, whether a parent approved ads. From version 2.1 it also keeps whether you play two players or against the AI, the AI’s level, and the last settings file. Version 2.1 also kept your streak, Rain Checks and Daily Question results, an estimate of how close your guesses usually land, the usual time you played the Daily and your reminder choice; version 2.2 deletes those when it starts. This data stays on your device, is not backed up by us anywhere, and is removed entirely when you delete the app.

Versions before 2.0

Squeeze 1.x has no ads. If you add a photo to your hero there, you choose it with Apple’s photo picker; it stays only on your device and is never uploaded. Its background photos are Creative Commons and public-domain images from Wikimedia Commons, credited on the Photo credits page; they ship inside the app and are never downloaded.

Third parties

From version 2.0, Squeeze includes the Google Mobile Ads SDK and Google’s consent tool. From version 2.1, the settings file is hosted on GitHub Pages. Age assurance (and, in version 2.1, reminders) uses Apple’s own on-device frameworks.

Children

Squeeze is a family game suitable for all ages, but it is not directed at children. Ads are non-personalized and restricted to content suitable for all ages. When Apple’s age assurance tells Squeeze that a player is under 13, ad requests are marked for child treatment, and from 13 to 17 for teen treatment. Squeeze does not collect personal information from children.
