Squeeze — Privacy Policy

Effective October 17, 2026

The short version

Squeeze itself collects nothing about you. There are no accounts, no sign-up and no analytics, and the game plays fully offline. From version 2.0, Squeeze shows an occasional ad between rounds and at the end of a match through Google AdMob, which receives limited device data to deliver those ads. Ads are non-personalized, Squeeze never asks to track you, and nothing is for sale inside the app. Versions before 2.0 show no ads and contain no ad software.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The Google Mobile Ads SDK runs inside the app. To show and measure ads and to prevent fraud, Google may receive your IP address (which indicates your approximate location), device identifiers, information about the ads you see and tap, and app diagnostics such as crash and performance data. Squeeze requests non-personalized ads only: ads are not based on your interests or your activity in other apps, Squeeze never asks for permission to track you, and ads are limited to content suitable for all ages. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google’s consent tool asks for your choice before any ad; you can change it at any time with the Privacy choices button on Squeeze’s home screen. Google processes this data under its own policies: Google Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Age assurance

Some places require apps to respect a player’s age and a parent’s approval (for example Texas, from June 2026). Squeeze asks Apple whether such a law applies to you, and only if it does, iOS asks whether to share your age range (for example 13 to 15) with Squeeze through Apple’s Declared Age Range feature. The age range stays on your device and is never sent to us. Squeeze uses it for two things only: to tell Google to treat ad requests as a child’s or a teen’s (Google receives that instruction, not your age range), and, when a parent must approve app changes, to keep ads off until the parent approves through Apple. Squeeze remembers only whether that approval was given.

Location (“Near You” mode)

If you choose the optional Near You mode, Squeeze asks for while-using location permission so it can pick trivia questions about places around you. Your coordinates are compared against a places database that ships inside the app, on your device. Your location is used only in the app's working memory, and only in that mode: it is never saved to your device, never transmitted, and never shared with anyone, including us and Google. Declining or revoking permission simply switches the app to Random mode.

Data stored on your device

Squeeze keeps a small amount of data locally so the game works: your language, your Random or Near You choice and match length, whether you have watched the demo round, the counters that space out ads (matches played, rounds since the last ad, the time of the last ad) and, where age assurance applies, whether a parent approved ads. This data stays on your device, is not backed up by us anywhere, and is removed entirely when you delete the app.

Versions before 2.0

Squeeze 1.x has no ads. If you add a photo to your hero there, you choose it with Apple’s photo picker; it stays only on your device and is never uploaded. Its background photos are Creative Commons and public-domain images from Wikimedia Commons, credited on the Photo credits page; they ship inside the app and are never downloaded.

Third parties

From version 2.0, Squeeze includes the Google Mobile Ads SDK and Google’s consent tool. Age assurance uses Apple’s own on-device frameworks.

Children

Squeeze is a family game suitable for all ages, but it is not directed at children. Ads are non-personalized and restricted to content suitable for all ages. When Apple’s age assurance tells Squeeze that a player is under 13, ad requests are marked for child treatment, and from 13 to 17 for teen treatment. Squeeze does not collect personal information from children.
